Laptop security incident report

Laptop “LAPTOP” (HP, Windows 11 Pro) · Incident: Saturday 24 October 2026 · Investigation: Monday 26 October 2026 · All times are local (Singapore Standard Time, UTC+8)

Verdict: the laptop is clean. The stolen accounts still need to be secured.

On 24 Oct at 3:46 PM, someone using the laptop ran a fake game installer. It was password-stealing malware. It was active for 2 hours 21 minutes until Microsoft Defender stopped it at 6:07 PM. In that time it copied saved browser passwords, login cookies, Discord tokens and 2FA codes from every browser profile on the laptop.

On 26 Oct at about 5:30 AM, attackers used that stolen data to take over several accounts (Instagram, two Gmail accounts, Claude, an alt Discord).

Two full investigations on 26 Oct, one with administrator access, found no malware left on the laptop.

2. Root cause

Current holder of the laptop tried to download a pirated copy of a paid game (Needy Streamer Overload) and was redirected to a malware site offering a fake “setup” zip with a key in its name. Defender blocked the first file, but the third attempt contained a brand-new malware installer (built hours earlier that day) that Defender did not recognise yet.

Contributing factor: several people's accounts were still logged in, with passwords saved, in this laptop's browsers, including the previous holder's accounts from before the laptop was handed over in August. The malware took data from every browser profile.

8. Limitations

No investigation can guarantee 100%. The malware was a “loader” that can download other programs. No trace of any was found, but the full Defender scan and an optional Offline scan are the final confirmation.

Windows' PowerShell activity logs have a fixed size, and the investigation's own activity filled them, overwriting entries older than the morning of 26 Oct. The key period (24 Oct, 3–7 PM) had already been reviewed. The malware used VBScript, which those logs don't record. All other logs are intact.

9. How to avoid this next time

  • A downloaded “setup” with a KEY or password in its name, or a site that tells you to turn off your antivirus, is malware.
  • Don't keep other people's accounts logged in on a shared laptop. Sign out before handing a device over.
  • Keep 2FA codes on your phone, not in a browser extension.
  • Use unique passwords (a password manager) and passkeys where offered.
  • Never add Defender exclusions for downloaded games or tools.
